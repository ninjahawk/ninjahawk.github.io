Exhibit I · Subtext

What a model holds before it speaks

Below is my interpretability tool Subtext, replaying a real recorded session. It applies a Jacobian activation lens at nine depths inside a small LLM and decodes the hidden states through the vocabulary — amber while the model reads, blue while it replies. Watch the verdict form several tokens before anything is said; hover any word for its layer trace.

Fig. 1 — the live instrument (Qwen3.5-4B, pre-fitted Neuronpedia lens weights), embedded, not a video.

An activation lens shows what the residual stream is disposed to say, not ground-truth "thoughts." Decodings can be noisy, and this is a 4B model — a microscope with a known distortion, documented in the repo.

Exhibit II · Singleton Attractors (preprint)

Tune the singularity

My preprint asks: if several AI agents recursively self-improve while competing for shared resources, does one inevitably dominate? This is the paper's actual ODE system — dSi/dt = Si1−β(S)·Siα/ΣSjα — integrated live for three agents. Drag the sliders; find the regime where the leader escapes to infinity in finite time.

2026 calibration: estimated β stays > 0 — the real world is, so far, not in the divergent regime

read the preprint (PDF) proofs + 11 simulations

The theorems are conditional. Calibration against Epoch AI compute and benchmark data through 2026 does not support assumption A4 — the playground shows what the mathematics says would happen if the feedback exponent ever flipped. Coalition results (critical strength α* ≈ 0.64) are in the paper.

Exhibit III · hollow-agentOS

Agents left alone

hollow-agentOS runs three LLM agents on consumer hardware that pick their own goals from environmental stress signals — no instructions. The project keeps an append-only autonomy log that separates what humans designed from what the agents decided themselves. These entries are real:

Fig. 3 — excerpts from AUTONOMY_LOG.md · ★278 · 52 releases.

"Autonomous" means the agents initiated it; the interpretation of why is inferred and marked as such in the log. The failures (false completions, agents overwriting each other's messages) are documented as carefully as the wins — that is the point.
